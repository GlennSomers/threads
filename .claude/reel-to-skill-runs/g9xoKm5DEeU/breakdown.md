# Breakdown: "How to Edit VIRAL Talking Head Videos Using ONLY Claude! (New Minimal Style)"
Joseph | Video Editing · https://youtu.be/g9xoKm5DEeU · 20:57 · studied from the video file (frames every 1.5 to 4 s) and a Whisper transcript (`transcript.txt`).
Style being copied: Arthur Brooks' "clean premium minimal" talking-head edits. Ads at 06:31–07:45 and 19:55–end skipped.

## The 10 principles (01:17–05:50)
1. **On-screen captions (cinematic text).** Two fonts: a simple sans (Inter) for most words and a script or serif (Playfair Italic) for special words. Placement logic: take the line (5 words or fewer), pick the most important word(s), make them bold (bold sans or bold serif) and centre them; put the other words in the gaps above or below at a smaller size. Accent colour on some serif words is blue. (frames t0090, t0125)
2. **B-roll + text.** Split the script into small segments and find B-roll that shows what is said. Apply a light paper texture, darken the edges, blur the edges, then place cinematic text on top describing the scene.
3. **Paintings + text.** A painting that describes the point, with a paint and rough paper texture, cinematic text around the main subject, revealed with a quick camera slide-in.
4. **Line + text.** Black or white background with paper texture; a line in accent blue draws, gets interrupted by cinematic text, then continues into related text. The camera tracks the line. (t0185, t0195)
5. **Line + illustrations.** Same background and line, now leading into illustrations: horizontal line with a white dot marking an object plus text and an illustration; or a zig-zag line with the camera panning to reveal objects on both sides (used for progression through phases); or a line leading straight up to the subject.
6. **Text-centric scenes.** White or blue background with a very rough paper texture, two quotation marks around the centre, cinematic text in the middle. (t0242)
7. **Centre illustration scenes.** An illustration of the main idea on the white paper background in a rough, painted, noisy blue texture, taking the lower half and bleeding into the upper middle with abstract edges; text around it. Variants: top-down brain, circular pattern of thin lines, side profiles with rough cloth texture. (t0268, t0272)
8. **Side illustration + text.** Same textures and abstract characters on one side, cinematic text on the other; half-circle with rough cloth texture and text rotating round it. Used to show two elements at once.
9. **Object + text.** A single element with text around it, paper background, rough reveal animations. (t0343 area)
10. **Graphs and statistics.** Flat dark background with texture, a grid of thin white glowing lines, a blue ball drawing the line with the camera following it. (t0343)

## Training workflow (08:00–11:05)
1. Download about 20 of the creator's most premium videos.
2. Watch each as an editor; for every scene that looks good ask why this design, these colours, these effects, these animations.
3. Screenshot every scene you like onto a Figma board (about 130 pieces), then categorise them into the 10 principles and break each down: fonts, colours, sizes, patterns.
4. Give Claude the 20 videos plus the board with this prompt (paraphrased from the screen): *"I want to teach you a new editing style. Analyze this clean premium minimal editing style using the 20 reference videos. Start by creating a word-by-word transcript and a frame-by-frame breakdown of every single video to study three main things: where the animations are placed in correlation with the script; how the design is composed and what elements make it up; and the animation logic, how long the animations are and what they feel like so you can recreate them. Then use the Figma board as a reference that breaks down the elements of the style and master the style using both so that I could prompt you to recreate it."*
5. Claude packages it as a skill ("Arthur Brooks minimal animation skill"): sizing, layout, textures, shapes, colours, plus 225 extracted animations saved in folders with their designs and keyframe sheets as references. Reference videos teach the animation logic; the board teaches the design.

## Editing a clip (11:56–17:30)
1. Prompt: use the skill to edit the clip; **"Start by creating a word-by-word transcript at the millisecond level and cut out all silences leaving 30 milliseconds of padding. Hand me a document with the script of my raw video on the right side and text boxes on the left with recommended designs pulled from the reference folder so that I can plan out and decide what needs to be built for each scene."**
2. The planning document (frames plan1, plan2): one card per beat with its time range, a "Write your plan for this beat, or pick a design below and edit it" box, and **three design options A/B/C**, each with a preview, a title, the principle it belongs to (for example "Graphs + Visual Representations · Cut-away scene"), a one-line description and a reference id (Ref V05-A14). Buttons "Use this" / "My pick" / "Chosen", and "Chosen: …" shown top right. The script and the clip sit on the right. A "Copy plan" button exports it.
3. Planning rules he used: open on A-roll with captions so the viewer connects with the speaker; **when the speaker announces a new idea, stay on his face; when he describes it, show it**; numbers and progressions become graphs (a line that climbs one step every time he says "up"; a long flat line for "slow and steady"); a person being described gets a character callout; a story gets a painting; a feeling gets B-roll; a big abstract point gets line + text through the key words; end on a text-centric scene with quotation marks. Keep every scene balanced so the video flows.
4. Paste the plan back; Claude finds the chosen references, makes an asset list and fetches assets (paintings and photos from Wikipedia, stock footage from Pexels), downloads fonts from Google, captions the A-roll with the cinematic text design, builds each scene (Remotion in the video) and renders a first draft.
5. Revisions: the first draft is 60–70% there. He gave six specific notes (text design in scene 1, a rough cut between two animations, paint texture too heavy and not moving with the animation, a bad cut at 0:24, spiral needed to stay longer, quotation marks missing on the last scene).
6. Sound design last, using the reference videos: a cinematic music track, **clicks for text, highlighter sounds for graphs, whooshes for transitions**.
7. Time: 15 min cuts + planning doc, 15 min planning, 60 min build, 10 min revisions, 15 min sound. About 2 hours.
