## Idea

I remember for the longest time, I just kept thinking about getting the next camera, the better camera, getting the full-frame camera, getting the one that did the 10-bit video, getting better lenses, lower apertures, higher range that I actually could capture photos from further away, and I wanted all of the constraints around me to be removed because I thought that's what I needed to be creative and to create freely. Then I went and I shot my friend's wedding, both the photo and the video, and I had the Sony ZV-E10 Mark 1, which was literally marketed as a vlogging camera. I had an 18 to 105 lens from Sony. Very good. Still love it. And I had my iPhone. So I recorded the entire video on my iPhone using the Black Magic Cam, dialed in the settings, got everything the way I wanted it, and used my Apple Log 2. Perfect. Just finished editing that video. Oh, yes, and I had my DJI Mic 2s with a shotgun microphone that I used for directional audio, and then I had the second one to capture the ambience, which meant that I could isolate left and right. If the audio for one person was better, I raised that audio. If the shotgun mic was more what I wanted, I raised that audio. What I'm trying to get at is constraints don't limit your creativity. They force your creativity to activate and to find ways around these barriers, which they aren't necessarily barriers, more hurdles, because once you step over it, you realize, "Oh, wait, no, that actually was really easy." That actually was more fulfilling than if I just plopped it down, hit a button, and it was done. It also forces you to think about different ways you can capture the moment and different ways you can go about capturing a feeling or making sure you get this or you include that. I think we too often use these constraints as an excuse to not do the thing when the constraints can actually propel you forward to do the thing. Just some food for thought.
